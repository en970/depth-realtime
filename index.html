Capture

View

The network reports inverse depth, so everything past a couple of metres compresses into the darkest few percent of the scale. This maps it logarithmically instead, giving equal ratios of distance equal shares of the scale. Measured on a room scene it takes the far third from ten distinct levels to seventy-nine.

Shows the estimate as geometry rather than as a picture of geometry. Drag to turn it; it drifts on its own when left alone. A wall that bulges or an object floating off its background is obvious here and invisible on a colour map.

Replaces the depth map with the camera image swayed by depth: near things move against far ones, which reads as three dimensions on a flat screen. Occluded edges stretch rather than tearing, so the cost is a soft smear at silhouettes.

A line at every fixed step of depth, as on a topographic map. A smooth ramp shows that two surfaces differ but not by how much; lines make it countable, and reveal gradients too shallow to see as colour.

Darkens the far side of each silhouette and shades hollows and ridges, so surface curvature reads as shape rather than as a flat band of colour. Applied as a bounded gain, so the colour scale still orders depth correctly.

Layout

Model

Runs the network at the resolution it was trained on and applies the guided upsampling in full. Roughly half the frame rate, noticeably more structure.

Square input to the network, constrained to multiples of 14. Higher values sharpen object boundaries and cost throughput.

Blends each depth frame with the previous one. Suppresses flicker; high values leave a motion trail.

How far smoothing backs off where the scene is moving. Off by default: measured on a panning scene it added flicker rather than removing trails, because a frame-to-frame difference carries as much noise as motion.

Steers the depth map by the camera image, so depth edges land on object outlines instead of being smeared across them by magnification. Too high on a strongly patterned surface can let texture bleed into the depth.

Lowers the input resolution when the measured frame time exceeds the budget, and raises it again once thermal headroom returns.
